# Funeral Afterparty at the Legion

Act 2, gig 05. The drummer, bassist and guitarist play all five songs in the Legion. Earlier that afternoon, the same people buried Dad. One wet shoulder takes us back there. By the time the uncle gets to make his toast, we’ve seen what it took for everyone to get into this room.

## 1. The Beatles — While My Guitar Gently Weeps

Before the first note, hear rain ticking into the metal umbrella bin. Start just behind the floor tom. From here the drummer can see the uncle smoothing his toast card, the dessert trolley parked on the parquet, and the daughter keeping the chair beside her clear. Establish this angle early so we can return to it as the room fills.

Stay close enough to the kit to see the tea towel jump on the snare. Across the room, the daughter silently mouths a bend in the guitar line, right down to the little movement of her jaw when the pitch comes down. She knows Dad’s record that well. During the outro she sets a folded request slip on the floor tom. Follow her glance to her uncle. One shoulder of his good black suit is soaked.

## 2. Bullet For My Valentine — A Place Where You Belong

On the opening, cut from the wet wool to the same shoulder under the casket earlier that day. Track beside the bearers at walking pace. A black umbrella keeps dipping across the lens. When the path narrows, Dad’s friend briefly loses sight of her wife under the overlapping umbrellas. Her wife reaches through and touches the back of her free hand.

At the first chorus, let the procession turn away from the camera. For the first time we can see the length of it, shoes picking their way through wet grass all the way back to the gate. Keep a few faces recognizable under the umbrellas. Dad’s friend wears a beautifully cut black suit and silver earrings. Her wife holds the umbrella high enough to clear her shoulder, getting rain down her own collar.

At the grave, frame the daughter in profile with the casket lid crossing the foreground. Show the straps take the weight. Hold one shot while the casket lowers. As the lid clears the frame, the daughter sees her dad’s friend looking back at her from across the grave. Let them have a few beats together. For the guitar lead, return to the band at the Legion. Two cousins release the trolley brakes and roll it aside. A clean square of parquet catches the window light.

## 3. Billy Talent — Cure for the Enemy

Start on the floor-tom angle again. Through the doorway beyond the kit, the friend in the black suit stands dripping. Her wife slips the jacket off her shoulders and frees the silver earring caught in its collar. Stay long enough to see the friend lean into her hand. The drummer can see them both from the stool.

At the first chorus, the daughter leaves her shoes under Dad’s chair and holds out both hands to her uncle. He has to pocket the toast card to take them. Frame their whole bodies for the first turn. She’s in stocking feet, he’s in stiff dress shoes, and they have to work out which of them is leading. Give their laugh time to land before cutting back to the kit.

## 4. The Requiem — I’ll Be Late For My Funeral

Give the opening accents to the player in a steady three-quarter shot of the band. On the verse, follow the uncle carrying Dad’s portrait from the table to the upright piano. He turns it toward the dancers and hangs his loosened tie over the corner. From the piano we can see his niece beckon him back.

At the next return to the kit, another folded napkin lands beside the daughter’s request. Pull focus past it to the friend and her wife stepping onto the parquet together. Keep the frame wide enough for the drummer to see the people they’re playing for.

## 5. My Chemical Romance — Helena

During the quiet opening, get low beside the empty chair. The uncle’s dress shoes have joined his niece’s. On the full-band rush, come up behind the drummer. The cousins pour through the gap where the dessert trolley stood. Hold this angle through the first chorus so the player gets to see the floor fill while playing it.

At the cutoff, a cousin takes a photo beneath the portrait. Catch the uncle halfway into frame, still trying to get his arm around his niece. In the quiet afterward he unfolds his toast card. She taps a spoon against a glass for him. End on the room turning to listen. For the garage reward, use that same photograph with her folded request slip around it.

## Cue and playback notes

Stage the cemetery as a flashback with the Legion performance continuing underneath. Use the shoulder cut to enter it and the guitar lead to bring the player home. Keep the drum lanes and their normal note travel visible across both cuts. Aim the grey cemetery sky and reflective casket hardware away from the lanes. Put the family photo flash after the final playable hit.

Treat the chorus and lead cues as musical landmarks. During chart review, mark the opening carry, the procession wide, the lowering and the return to the kit against the selected Bullet recording. Play through the lowering at full speed to check that the lid clears the daughter’s sightline and the two faces register before the cut. Check the opening and closing floor-tom shots from the same camera position with the note highway active.
