# Drumsmith story mode setlists

The first time anyone plays along with you, it’s a commuter tapping a travel mug. Four hours later the same rhythm comes back from a stadium stand. By the thirteenth booking, that drummer has to give a count that an entire orbital concert hall can follow.

**Passing a booking.** In sets 01–12, finish the five tracks in the printed order. Bank each finished track’s accuracy, then compare the mean of the five percentages with the set’s pass mark. A replay can raise a banked best. Resume at the next unfinished song. If all five are banked below the mark, replay any of them. Award the room move, item and audition once on passing. The next set opens after the previous pass and any required audition choice. The optional keys seat can be left empty. Passing the stadium rolls the credits and ends the story. After the credits, set 13 opens as a post-credits bonus. The Hall of Fame judges one uninterrupted performance of all five songs, and a passing run plays the induction as an epilogue.

The existing gig system runs a set back to back and checks its average accuracy. Leaving abandons that run. Story banking and this manifest are a proposed campaign layer. Free play stays open to every library song. Backyard Party, Club Night, Festival Slot and Arena Headline remain independent side gigs with generated setlists.

**Staging.** Each booking has one gig venue under the note highway. The director catches the change in a wide, band or crowd shot at a marked musical section. Keep the action high or to either side of the highway’s lower centre. At song end, advance the venue state even if the director missed the shot. Sets 01–12 restore it on resume. Set 13 carries it only within the current attempt. The first gig uses the hideout basement itself. From then on, the hideout is where the band lives between dates and where the souvenirs accumulate.

**Drum difficulty.** The campaign uses Expert drums. The two numbers on each booking are provisional targets for the median track density in the set, in drum notes per second. Measure each new chart as note count divided by track duration. Openers can sit below the band and closers can go above it. Check the measured medians across bookings when the charts land. The playing notes describe the records’ rhythmic demands. The early acoustic opener especially leaves real space for rests. Kit changes are visual and use the same drum lanes.

All 65 primaries are slated for new campaign charts. Each pair of alternates comes from the saved-song export and fits that slot’s musical or dramatic job. An alternate is an editorial option before a booking ships. If picked, check its density and bind the cue to a section in that recording. The printed primary determines the set time.

**Building the band.** An audition offers two written candidates. Choose one for each required seat and adjust their look afterward if desired. Selected members keep their appearance at home and onstage. Leave unfilled seats clear. Fixed guest acts play the indicated session dates. Candidates who took another gig return in the specified wing, opening bill or crowd shot.

The [campaign manifest](Story-Mode-Setlists.json) lists the exact recordings, both alternates, guest assignments and cumulative opening and closing flags for every track. A missing section marker moves the visual cue to song end.

## 01  In the Basement with My AirPods

The old bucket drums are still beside the real kit. Upstairs somebody leaves the kitchen door open a little longer with each song. Down here it’s just you, the camcorder and the BASSIST WANTED flyer on the gig board.

**Venue and kit.** Basement base, low build cost. Use the built hideout basement as the playable room. Dress the existing bucket, camcorder, CRT, window well and kitchen stairs. Play the hand-me-down kit on the carpet offcut.
