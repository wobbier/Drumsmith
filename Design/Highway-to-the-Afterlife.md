# Highway to the Afterlife

Five songs follow one band from a highway chase to an audience with God. On Earth, the whole trip takes only as long as the rescue. The highway cop is the one person who sees the band before the crash and is still there when they wake up.

**Playback.** Starting the set starts one continuous show. The last playable note of each chart leads into the next recording, even across a change of venue; a song can hold the hand-off for an outro of its own (Kickstart My Heart's, below). Save the results screen and the decision about what to play next for after the rescue. Campaign placement and the rules for resuming an interrupted run are open.

**Recordings.** The set is listed under `ProposedSets` in `Story-Mode-Setlists.json`, so it is outside the campaign until it is placed. Every song has a stand-in (a click and a rock beat) in `Assets/DLC/Story/<Artist - Title>/`, tied to the recording by its Spotify link. Put the real song in a folder of its own directly under `Assets/DLC` (Convert Custom DLC reads only those, not `Story/`; Opus stems need Ogg copies first) and give its `TrackData.txt` the same Spotify link; the story takes it over the stand-in. Free Bird and Bat Country share their stand-ins with the Stadium and Steel Mill sets, so they must stay the same recordings.

## 1. Mötley Crüe — Kickstart My Heart

**Opening.** Start low on a trailer wheel spinning against the lane markings. Travel up past the edge of the flatbed to the kick pedal. When the band comes in, pull out far enough to see the whole kit rattling on the deck and the police lights closing behind it. Give the player a clear view of the first notes while the shot opens up.

**The chase.** The drummer sits toward the front of the trailer's deck, facing back down the road at the cops; the guitarist stands on the cab's roof in a power stance, facing back over the trailer. The trailer swings a little on the fifth wheel through the road's curves, both bodies ride their springs over the bumps (and slam down out of the jump), and the band bounce with them. Track alongside the singer at the wheel during the first verse, climb the passenger door to the bassist on the first chorus, and arc above the roof guitarist during the solo. At the bridge, swing past the driver’s mirror with the oncoming Pacific Freight cabover. In the cruiser, shoot the cop head-on through the windshield. He keeps his eyes on the trailer, checks his radio, and gives one small involuntary nod in time; from then on his head goes with the band on every beat, and from the guitar solo (`heli_again`) the whole pursuit's are going too. In the helicopter view, start wide enough to see both vehicles, then let the camera operator zoom past the trailer and correct back onto the drummer. That imperfect zoom makes it feel like someone really caught this on camera.

**Road and wreck.** Loop the highway in straight and gently curving sections. Use passing light poles and changes of angle to disguise the join. Reserve one distinct stretch of shoulder for the wreck so the player will recognize it in the finale. Its billboard (PREPARE TO MEET GOD, NEXT EXIT) is seen on the way: billboards go by with the road every twenty seconds, HELL IS REAL and that one in turn, and the one the rig stops under is there from the wreck. In the closing bars the trailer slews across a lane. Let the player finish the last hits with the kit still in view. Cut to the helicopter as the trailer crashes and erupts in flame: in the road a few seconds on, the rig over on its side at the right edge and through the guardrail, the band thrown onto the asphalt beside it, the pursuit pulled up behind across the lanes (the same layout as Free Bird's crash site, `CRASH_LYING` and `COP_CAR` in the generator). Down at road level, the band lies dead on the asphalt, one drumstick just beyond the drummer’s fingers; the bodies are the band's own (dressed as whoever plays each part, and the set is one cast from Kickstart to My Hero).

**The outro.** The recording ends a second after the last note; the show doesn't. For sixteen seconds after it the venue plays on by its own sound: the trailer going over, the blast, the fire, ears ringing and the pursuit's sirens. The helicopter holds wide on the burning rig for seven of them, then the road-level shot of the bodies takes the rest.

**Into Free Bird.** Stay beside that hand as the next recording starts. The first movement belongs to a second, translucent hand lifting away while the physical one stays on the road.

## 2. Lynyrd Skynyrd — Free Bird

**Crash site.** Give the slow opening time to breathe. The drummer gets to their feet, reaches for the dropped stick, and passes straight through it. Across the shoulder, the others have stood up too. A wide shot finally shows both versions of the entire band. The cop runs into frame and kneels beside the body the drummer just left. Follow the drummer’s gaze down to him before leaving Earth.

**The tease.** They don't fall at first: they rise. With the first verse their feet leave the road and the crash site drops away under them, the cop still at the body. The cloud base comes down to them as the night turns (the sky's own cloud: it closes round them for a few seconds, then opens, and they rise up a gap in it, its walls going by and the Earth still there down the gap, the crash burning; they come out over its tops at dawn with a sea of heaped cloud under them, the ground showing through its breaks, thunderheads standing up out of it far off toward the horizon, rising slowly from there). At the first chorus a light opens overhead, gold, and grows through the verses that follow. At the last chorus the gates themselves start down to meet them, standing open, and the camera leaves the band as a whole: each line of it is one of them alone, close and from below, their face to one side of the picture and the light over them. The singer on "And this bird you'll never change", the guitarist on "And a bird you cannot change", the bassist on "And this bird you cannot change". On "Lord knows I can't change" it is the drummer at the kit, and the camera tilts up off them into the light: the gates are close over them now. On "Lord help me I can't change" we see through the singer's eyes. Their own pale hand goes up ahead of them into the light, open, straining, the light just past the fingertips and its rays pouring down past the fingers. Then the hand turns flat across it and shuts it out.

**Denied.** That is what does it. As the tempo breaks, the light goes out at a stroke and a red bolt comes down in its place. "Lord I can't change" is the top of the climb: wide and held, with room round them, they hang there under the red, stop, and turn over, and the day starts to run backwards. They drop into the cloud. "Won't you fly-y" is the free fall: head first out of the bottom of the cloud, the air tearing up past them in streaks, and under them the moonlit land, the road, and the crash they left still burning, coming up. On "Free bird" we are back on the ground at the crash, low by the burning truck, the road lit by its fire, as they come down out of the night sky like a falling star. On "yeah" they smash through the road (a flash, the asphalt bursting up round them), and as the guitar riff takes off they are falling into the first circle.

**Descent.** The tumble is over before they reach the road: they turned over at the top of the climb, tumbled past one another on the way down, and pulled into a head-first dive with their ghostly instruments beside them, so the drummer is already playing when the guitar solo takes off.

**The nine circles.** The solo is the fall, and the fall is Dante's: they smash through the road on its first riff and are straight into Limbo, with only a few seconds of the shaft's throat between, then each circle of hell in turn, one to a passage of the solo, each with its own rock, light and weather, named on screen as it closes round them. The fall rips along with the guitar and gets faster circle by circle: 45 m/s through Limbo, 52 through Lust, then 59, 66, 73, 81, 89, 97 and 105 m/s in the ice, the streaks, embers and snow racing up past them as much faster. The circles are stacked, as the pit is, and nothing cuts or pops between them: the next one comes up from under the one they are in while that one goes on up overhead, one move of five seconds, with a breath of bare rock between. Its ledges wind down the wall all the way round, each one whole turn of a spiral with its end under its own start (behind the venue's own cameras, on the audience side, it narrows to a bare path along the wall). The band zips past; the circles' own people get the camera, and they answer the playing. Limbo's shades crowd both its ledges in droves to watch them go by, packed two deep along the lip and more against the wall: wallflowers, swaying and holding back among the columns while the drums are wrong (the lip left bare), coming forward to the lip and dancing, arms up, while they are right. In Lust the lustful swim up the wall against the wind that drags them down; while the playing is good they give it up and dance in the storm, while it is bad the storm flings them every way, and the hurricane whirling round the band whips its souls round faster and carries some of them off up the shaft. The gluttons sit fat in their mire under the rain: for a bad player sunk to the chin in it, feeding, hardly moving; for a good one up out of it to the belly, arms in the air, bouncing to the beat. Cerberus sits on Gluttony's lower ledge: the band catches his eye, and over his shoulder he stops dead, all three heads cocked and ears up, like a dog, as they drop past his noses; then he is off the ledge after them and runs head first down the shaft beside them, round the outside of the fall, keeping level with the band until the circle carries him off, and dropping behind them while the drums go wrong. In Greed the hoarders and wasters shove their boulders (about as high as they are) up the spiral of the ledge, heaving on every crash and kick the player hits: an even column in step while the playing is good, a rabble that straggles, bumps into each other and slips back on every miss while it isn't. In Wrath the spiral is a river, the Styx, and the wrathful in it are a pool party gone to a mosh pit, rubber rings bobbing among them, shoving each other about on the beat while the player hits and shuffling sullenly while they miss. Then the city of Dis: its iron wall and towers red hot, the fallen angels jeering from the battlements, and iron maidens hung out over the drop on long chains from gibbets along the wall, the screaming face of one of the damned in the window of each and his hands clawing out of her sides. While the playing is good the maidens swing to the music; while it is bad they hang still, red hot from the feet up, over the heretics' open tombs, whose fires flare up the chains at them and leap higher at every miss. Violence has Dante's three rings on each of its terraces: at the back, against the wall, the wood of the suicides, dead grey trees twisted like rags that the harpies tear at; in the middle Phlegethon, the river of boiling blood with fire running on it, the murderers and thugs in it to the waist; at the lip the burning sand, its souls burnt to the bone. Played well, the trees lash out and drive the harpies off into the air over the river, the souls in the blood rock out and the skeletons get up and dance; played badly, the harpies are in the trees savaging them, the souls go under the blood and the skeletons lie dead. Malebolge is all ten of its ditches, one under another down the spiral twelve metres apart, each its own colour so that each reads as it flashes past: the panderers and seducers filing two ways under the whips of horned demons, who crack them on the beat; the flatterers sunk in dung; the simoniacs upside down in holes in the rock, kicking, their soles on fire; the sorcerers and diviners in blue with pointed hats, their heads wrung round so their beards hang down their backs; the grafters in the black boiling pitch, the Malebranche on the lip jabbing their hooks at them on the beat; the hypocrites bowed under hooded cloaks of gilded lead, Caiaphas staked across their road; the thieves among the green serpents, wound in them or bitten; the false counsellors, each a dark shape in a tall flame (Ulysses and Diomedes in one with two tips); the sowers of discord split open and spilling, Bertran de Born carrying his own head, and the demon who splits them chopping with his sword; and the falsifiers, rotten with scabs, lying in heaps and scratching. The demons work whatever the playing; the sinners groove along only for a good player. Last, the cold, brief: the chained giants round the well, then Cocytus, terrace under terrace of ice with the traitors in it deeper the further down they go, chest deep in Caina, to the shoulders in Antenora, to the chin with their faces turned up in Ptolomea, and in Judecca only a face and a hand left out of it (twice the size of the others, so that they read). Frozen, they still rock out for a good player, heads banging, a hand up out of the ice making the horns; for a bad one they are stiff, the hands clawing at nothing. They press on down and land in the frozen heart. Leave the swap to itself. The looking is done from inside the circles, twice in each once it is round them: cut to a camera riding up with the rock among the damned, framed to one side clear of the note highway, as the band streaks down the shaft and is gone: in Limbo low along the lip of the colonnade, the shades packed two deep down the line of it, a wall of sad watchers, then over it; in Lust in the thick of the swimmers; in Greed side on to one team of pushers mid-heave, then along the whole column winding up the slope. In Lust the camera then goes round the band with the hurricane for seven seconds, as one of the lustful; in Gluttony the second look is over Cerberus's shoulder as he watches them go by, and the third follows him down the shaft after them. Below Wrath the looks go to the people of each circle: in Dis, out among the chains beside a maiden, the face at her window in the near third and the line of them going on round the ring, then looking down past the maidens to the tombs' fires under them; in Violence, from behind the wood, its pale twisted trees and the harpies against the glowing river, then on Phlegethon's bank by the skeletons on the sand; in Malebolge, over the pitch with a Malebranche and his hook filling the near side, then in the first ditch with a demon's whip going; in Cocytus, close over Caina's frozen traitors rocking out, then down among the faces of Judecca.

**Arrival in hell.** Out of the bottom of the ninth circle at 8:13, with fifty-three seconds of the song left, they land in the heart of hell, and it is frozen. The kick drum hits snow on the island and throws it up round the kit, frost blowing past. Go wide from over the band: the stands rise round the floor in nine tiers, one for each circle they fell through, all of it under snow and every demon in them frozen stiff in a statue of ice; the lake is a sheet of ice; and at the head of the staircase Satan, as big as the stands, sits frozen to the waist into a block of ice on his rock-and-roll throne (amp cabinets for its arms and its back, a fan of iron spikes over it). He has Dante's three faces on the one head: red in front, yellow-white on his right, black on his left. His wings beat on and on, and their wind is what has frozen hell over: snow and frost come down the stairs at the band. Go close on the three faces. The band can see exactly who decides whether they get out.

**The thaw.** The playing brings him round. Six seconds before the solo's last passage a glow kindles in his chest, his heart, beating with the song. On that passage the ice cracks red and starts to melt back off him, and he nods; the demons on the floor thaw, then the tiers above them one at a time from the floor up, each statue sliding off a demon who is already dancing. Eight seconds on, the next three tiers, the lake's ice breaks up over the lava and he rocks his shoulders; nine more, the top tiers, the braziers catch and he air-drums along with the player's own hits. How much he and the thawed crowd move answers the playing; the thaw itself keeps to the song, so that it is always done by the end. On the big ending it is fire and brimstone at a stroke: the lake erupts, the lava falls and the fire jets go, the bats are back, the light goes red, and the spirits they fell past drop into the stands for the party. Follow the stairs up past demon babes on the balustrades and carved infernal figures to him, then go high and wide over the lake.

**Into Bat Country.** On the ending, the crowd turns toward the throne. Satan lifts his right hand toward the band and rolls it at them, twice, for another song. The drummer looks across the kit to the guitarist. The next track answers him immediately.

## 3. Avenged Sevenfold — Bat Country

**The encore.** It is played in the fire, the hell everyone thinks of, with Satan as its host. Start from the throne looking down, over his shoulder. Satan can take in the whole stage from his seat, but the drummer has to look up between fills to see his face. With each return to the riff, move one step closer to him: head-on at the first chorus, from behind the kit at the second verse, from halfway up the stairs at the third. Let the crowd loosen up first. One demon on the steps catches a fill with both hands on the stone, then turns around to see whether Satan noticed.

**Winning him over.** Frame Satan head-on at the same distance as the cop behind the windshield. Hold long enough to catch him nodding along. Later, give a drum fill to the player in a steady band shot, then cut to the throne for Satan trying to finish it on the arms of the throne, his fists coming down on the player's hits. By the closing passage he is on his feet, the whole height of him. Now the band can enjoy the encore alongside the crowd.

**Into Stairway to Heaven.** Satan picks up a phone beside the throne and looks straight up. His proposed line is “You gotta hear these guys.” Place it in space around the ending, or play it as a silent gesture if the recording leaves too little room. He lifts his free hand. The drummer’s boots leave the stone and smoke rises past the lens, turning white as Heart’s opening begins.

**Backup song.** Atreyu’s *Right Side of the Bed*. If it replaces *Bat Country*, use the first clean chorus for a front view of the drummer singing and cut to Satan’s answering head bob. Keep the same call upstairs.

## 4. Heart — Stairway to Heaven

**Ascent.** Use Heart’s performance with the choir. Begin inches from the side of the drummer’s face. Focus on the eyelashes while cloud and the other players slip in and out of focus behind them. Repeat the angle on another member looking down for the friend who fell beside them. Let their eyes meet. Save the first view of the horizon until the opening has had time to feel quiet and personal.

**Drum entrance.** During the opening stretch of rests, the drum highway can fade away completely. Cue its return from the first authored drum note, allowing the usual full note travel time before the strike. Time the camera’s pullback to that entrance. The band touches down on a floor of cloud. The kit settles first, then its reflection or shadow gives the player a sense of solid ground.

**The choir.** Ahead of the band, broad stairs rise to God’s platform. At the choir entrance, a shape the player could have mistaken for a distant arch opens its eyes. Pull wider to reveal interlocking rings and banks of wings, then wider again to show an entire choir of these angels. Keep the band and kit visible at the bottom of the composition as a scale reference. Give the choir a sustained wide shot so the player can take in how far it extends past the platform.

**Into My Hero.** As Heart’s performance resolves, the angels turn toward the drummer. God waits on the platform. Bring the camera all the way back from the celestial wide to a close shot of the sticks. Have the lanes ready for the exposed drum opening of the final song.

## 5. Foo Fighters — My Hero

**Opening drums.** Stay with the drummer in heaven through the opening pattern. The performance to God is the song: every cut away from it is to Earth, and each cut to Earth is the next thing that happens to the band there. Eight of them, in order, and never two in a row: every one comes back to heaven before the next.

**1. CPR.** At the guitar entrance, match a downward stick stroke to the cop’s hands giving CPR on the road. Close, slow motion, the trailer still burning behind him, red and blue across his uniform. Show his face clearly enough to recognize the driver from the chase.

**The sheet.** Partway through the first verse, between the CPR and the ambulance. The same road; a medic has the compressions now. Over the cop’s shoulder, his partner holds up a sheet off the dispatch with INNOCENT stamped across it (WRONG VEHICLE, CALL OFF PURSUIT in the type under it). The camera goes back behind the cop’s head as he looks up from it, and the focus goes out to the drummer under the medic’s hands, the ambulances’ lights behind them.

**2. The ambulance.** First chorus. The drummer is on a gurney going head first into the back of an ambulance, still in slow motion, a medic pushing and another waiting inside. The cop stands a step behind with a hand out.

**3. The highway.** Second chorus. The ambulance at speed inside an escort of cruisers, lightbars going, a helicopter’s light on its roof, traffic pulled over on the shoulder. Start on the road ahead of the lead cars and go up and over them.

**4. Inside.** The build-up into the last verse. Inside the ambulance, handheld and taking every bump: a medic rubbing the paddles together face to face, then holding them over the drummer’s chest and bringing them down; the cop on the bench bent over with his face in his hands, unable to watch; a medic breathing for them; the heart monitor on the wall over the drummer, turned to the camera, a flat line; the escort’s lights in the back windows, and everything on a hook or a strap swinging. The shock lands on the downbeat the band comes back in on. The drummer lifts off the cot and the cop looks up. From there the flat line on the monitor jumps on every beat of the song: their heart is keeping the band’s time. Back to heaven.

**God’s answer.** At the final chorus, God comes down the stairs to the band’s level. The angels fold their wings aside and the drummer can see the way home through the opening between them.

**5. The corridor.** Once the wings have parted. All four of them rushed down a hospital corridor on gurneys, shot from the foot of the drummer’s with the camera rocking on it: the ceiling lights going over, the cop and a nurse with the drip running alongside, the others in a line behind. Back to heaven for the final playable phrase, so the player finishes the performance that earns their return.

**The theatre.** After the corridor and one more return to heaven, 13 s before the cutoff. An operating theatre: from over the drummer’s chest, down onto the face under the mask between the surgeons’ heads; then back down the table and up to the whole room. The lamps, the team in scrubs, the monitor beating in time with the song, the guitarist’s theatre through the glass, and the cop at the window of the doors, as near as they will let him.

**6. The ward.** On the cutoff, daylight. A ward with a window the whole width of its far wall and four beds along the side walls, heads to the wall. Each of the band is sitting up in theirs their own way (upright with hands in the lap, lying back on the raised head of the bed, leaning forward over the knees, back with a hand behind the head), turned to look out at the sky: broken cloud going over and a low sun in the middle of the window. The cop sits in a chair by the back row, looking out with them; a monitor at every bed beats in time. The ward is eight storeys up (a hospital is tall) and its sill is low, so the town is looked down on: roofs under the horizon from 150 m out, a stone church with a spire in its yard among them, parks and street corners of trees (MitchTree recipes), a few towers and the hills standing above the horizon. Shoot it from behind them, from far enough back that all four are in the picture from the first frame on any screen from 4:3 up, and draw slowly back to the door as the song rings out. It is the end of the journey: heaven, hell and home. End the set there.

## Running the show

- Prepare the next recording, drum chart and opening view while the current song plays. Keep each chart aligned to its own recording. Use the held crash shot, throne gesture, rising smoke and close-up of the sticks to cover the four handoffs. Preserve musical intros and tails. A camera cue that runs late can cut directly to the next opening pose.
- Run each song’s scoring through its final playable note and carry the result forward in the background. Keep the drum highway readable across cuts, especially during the fall, the explosion and the flashing rescue lights. Restore normal lane visibility before every playable entrance.
- Bind cues to sections in the selected recordings once the charts are available. In particular, check how much solo remains after the landing in hell and how much lead-in Heart’s drummer gives the player. Playtest all four boundaries in one run and check that the first note after each handoff is visible and audible on time.

**Open decision — interruption and resume.** A one-sitting version would restart at the chase after the player leaves. A resumable version could reopen at a song boundary or at a saved position within a song. Song-boundary resume would restore that song’s opening tableau and the earlier results. Mid-song resume would also need to restore the matching audio position, chart and score state, camera sequence, and current condition of the band, then give the player a playable lead-in. Decide the checkpoint granularity after playing the full sequence. In every version, a player who keeps playing moves straight into the next song.
