# Highway to the Afterlife

Five songs follow one band from a highway chase to an audience with God. On Earth, the whole trip takes only as long as the rescue. The highway cop is the one person who sees the band before the crash and is still there when they wake up.

**Playback.** Starting the set starts one continuous show. The last playable note of each chart leads directly into the next recording, even across a change of venue. Save the results screen and the decision about what to play next for after the rescue. Campaign placement and the rules for resuming an interrupted run are open.

**Recordings.** The set is listed under `ProposedSets` in `Story-Mode-Setlists.json`, so it is outside the campaign until it is placed. Every song has a stand-in (a click and a rock beat) in `Assets/DLC/Story/<Artist - Title>/`, tied to the recording by its Spotify link. Put the real song in a folder of its own directly under `Assets/DLC` (Convert Custom DLC reads only those, not `Story/`; Opus stems need Ogg copies first) and give its `TrackData.txt` the same Spotify link; the story takes it over the stand-in. Free Bird and Bat Country share their stand-ins with the Stadium and Steel Mill sets, so they must stay the same recordings.

## 1. Mötley Crüe — Kickstart My Heart

**Opening.** Start low on a trailer wheel spinning against the lane markings. Travel up past the edge of the flatbed to the kick pedal. When the band comes in, pull out far enough to see the whole kit rattling on the deck and the police lights closing behind it. Give the player a clear view of the first notes while the shot opens up.

**The chase.** Keep coming back to three angles. On the trailer, shoot a band member bracing a foot against a monitor while leaning into a phrase. In the cruiser, shoot the cop head-on through the windshield. He keeps his eyes on the trailer, checks his radio, and gives one small involuntary nod in time. In the helicopter view, start wide enough to see both vehicles, then let the camera operator zoom past the trailer and correct back onto the drummer. That imperfect zoom makes it feel like someone really caught this on camera.

**Road and wreck.** Loop the highway in straight and gently curving sections. Use passing light poles and changes of angle to disguise the join. Reserve one distinct stretch of shoulder for the wreck so the player will recognize it in the finale. In the closing bars the trailer slews across a lane. Let the player finish the last hits with the kit still in view. Cut to the helicopter as the trailer crashes and erupts in flame. Down at road level, the band lies dead on the asphalt, one drumstick just beyond the drummer’s fingers.

**Into Free Bird.** Stay beside that hand as the next recording starts. The first movement belongs to a second, translucent hand lifting away while the physical one stays on the road.

## 2. Lynyrd Skynyrd — Free Bird

**Crash site.** Give the slow opening time to breathe. The drummer gets to their feet, reaches for the dropped stick, and passes straight through it. Across the shoulder, the others have stood up too. A wide shot finally shows both versions of the entire band. The cop runs into frame and kneels beside the body the drummer just left. Follow the drummer’s gaze down to him before leaving Earth.

**The tease.** They don't fall at first: they rise. With the first verse their feet leave the road and the crash site drops away under them, the cop still at the body. Cloud comes down round them in the moonlight. At the first chorus a light opens overhead, gold, and grows through the verses that follow. At the last chorus ("fly high, free bird") the gates themselves come down to meet them, standing open. They are a few seconds from going through.

**Denied.** Three seconds before the tempo breaks, the light goes out at a stroke and a red bolt comes down in its place. They drop. Look straight down with them at the Earth coming back up: the lane lines, the fire, the wreck. They go through the road on the first beat of the fast part, and the road is a slit of light shrinking overhead.

**Descent.** As the playing gathers speed, widen out. The members tumble past one another, reach out, and pull themselves back into something like their positions on the trailer. Bring their ghostly instruments into view so the drummer is already playing when the guitar solo takes off.

**The nine circles.** The solo is the fall, and the fall is Dante's: the shaft opens into each circle of hell in turn, one to a passage of the solo, each with its own rock, light and weather, named on screen as it closes round them. The circles are stacked, as the pit is, and nothing cuts or pops between them: the next one comes up from under the one they are in while that one goes on up overhead, one move, with a breath of bare rock between. Its ledges wind down the wall as they go round, and the damned on them move with the song, dipping and nodding in their own time. Limbo's grey colonnades and the shades under them. The hurricane of the lustful, whirling round the band. The gluttons in their mire under the rain, and Cerberus coming up out of the dark behind the drummer. The hoarders and wasters rolling their weights through walls of gold. The wrathful fighting in the Styx. The iron wall of Dis and the burning tombs. The river of blood, the wood of the suicides and the burning sand. The ditches of Malebolge with their fiends. Last, the cold: chained giants and the traitors frozen in Cocytus. Leave the swap to itself; once a circle is round them, give it one slow look at what stands on its ledges.

**Arrival in hell.** They reach the floor only as the song comes to its end, since the whole of the next song is played there. The kick drum hits stone at the foot of a staircase on the first crash of the ending. Follow the stairs up past demon babes on the balustrades and carved infernal figures to Satan seated at the top, then go wide: the stands rise round the floor in nine tiers, one for each circle they fell through. The band can see exactly who decides whether they get out.

**Into Bat Country.** On the ending, the crowd turns toward the throne. Satan rolls one hand toward the band for another song. The drummer looks across the kit to the guitarist. The next track answers him immediately.

## 3. Avenged Sevenfold — Bat Country

**The encore.** Start from the throne looking down. Satan can take in the whole stage from his seat, but the drummer has to look up between fills to see his face. With each return to the riff, move one step closer to him. Let the crowd loosen up first. One demon on the steps catches a fill with both hands on the stone, then turns around to see whether Satan noticed.

**Winning him over.** Frame Satan head-on at the same distance as the cop behind the windshield. Hold long enough to catch him nodding along. Later, give a drum fill to the player in a steady band shot, then cut to the throne for Satan trying to finish it on the armrests. By the closing passage he is on his feet. Now the band can enjoy the encore alongside the crowd.

**Into Stairway to Heaven.** Satan picks up a phone beside the throne and looks straight up. His proposed line is “You gotta hear these guys.” Place it in space around the ending, or play it as a silent gesture if the recording leaves too little room. He lifts his free hand. The drummer’s boots leave the stone and smoke rises past the lens, turning white as Heart’s opening begins.

**Backup song.** Atreyu’s *Right Side of the Bed*. If it replaces *Bat Country*, use the first clean chorus for a front view of the drummer singing and cut to Satan’s answering head bob. Keep the same call upstairs.

## 4. Heart — Stairway to Heaven

**Ascent.** Use Heart’s performance with the choir. Begin inches from the side of the drummer’s face. Focus on the eyelashes while cloud and the other players slip in and out of focus behind them. Repeat the angle on another member looking down for the friend who fell beside them. Let their eyes meet. Save the first view of the horizon until the opening has had time to feel quiet and personal.

**Drum entrance.** During the opening stretch of rests, the drum highway can fade away completely. Cue its return from the first authored drum note, allowing the usual full note travel time before the strike. Time the camera’s pullback to that entrance. The band touches down on a floor of cloud. The kit settles first, then its reflection or shadow gives the player a sense of solid ground.

**The choir.** Ahead of the band, broad stairs rise to God’s platform. At the choir entrance, a shape the player could have mistaken for a distant arch opens its eyes. Pull wider to reveal interlocking rings and banks of wings, then wider again to show an entire choir of these angels. Keep the band and kit visible at the bottom of the composition as a scale reference. Give the choir a sustained wide shot so the player can take in how far it extends past the platform.

**Into My Hero.** As Heart’s performance resolves, the angels turn toward the drummer. God waits on the platform. Bring the camera all the way back from the celestial wide to a close shot of the sticks. Have the lanes ready for the exposed drum opening of the final song.

## 5. Foo Fighters — My Hero

**Opening drums.** Stay with the drummer in heaven through the opening pattern. The performance to God is the song: every cut away from it is to Earth, and each cut to Earth is the next thing that happens to the band there. Six of them, in order, never the same place twice, and never two in a row: every one comes back to heaven before the next.

**1. CPR.** At the guitar entrance, match a downward stick stroke to the cop’s hands giving CPR on the road. Close, slow motion, the trailer still burning behind him, red and blue across his uniform. Show his face clearly enough to recognize the driver from the chase.

**2. The ambulance.** First chorus. The drummer is on a gurney going head first into the back of an ambulance, still in slow motion, a medic pushing and another waiting inside. The cop stands a step behind with a hand out.

**3. The highway.** Second chorus. The ambulance at speed inside an escort of cruisers, lightbars going, a helicopter’s light on its roof, traffic pulled over on the shoulder. Start on the road ahead of the lead cars and go up and over them.

**4. Inside.** The build-up into the last verse. Inside the ambulance, handheld and taking every bump: paddles on the drummer’s chest, the cop on the bench, a medic breathing for them, the escort’s lights in the back windows, and everything on a hook or a strap swinging. The shock lands on the downbeat the band comes back in on. The drummer lifts off the cot and the cop looks up. From there the flat line on the monitor jumps on every beat of the song: their heart is keeping the band’s time. Back to heaven.

**God’s answer.** At the final chorus, God comes down the stairs to the band’s level. The angels fold their wings aside and the drummer can see the way home through the opening between them.

**5. The corridor.** Once the wings have parted. All four of them rushed down a hospital corridor on gurneys, shot from the foot of the drummer’s with the camera rocking on it: the ceiling lights going over, the cop and a nurse with the drip running alongside, the others in a line behind. Back to heaven for the final playable phrase, so the player finishes the performance that earns their return.

**6. The ward.** On the cutoff, daylight. A ward with a window the whole width of its far wall and four beds along the side walls, heads to the wall. Each of the band is sitting up in theirs their own way (upright with hands in the lap, lying back on the raised head of the bed, leaning forward over the knees, back with a hand behind the head), turned to look out at the sky: broken cloud going over and a low sun in the middle of the window. The cop sits in a chair by the back row, looking out with them; a monitor at every bed beats in time. Below the sky, out of the window: the trees on the hospital’s lawn (MitchTree recipes), standing taller at the sides than under the sun, the town’s streets beyond them and hills behind. Shoot it from behind them, from far enough back that all four are in the picture from the first frame on any screen from 4:3 up, and draw slowly back to the door as the song rings out. It is the end of the journey: heaven, hell and home. End the set there.

## Running the show

- Prepare the next recording, drum chart and opening view while the current song plays. Keep each chart aligned to its own recording. Use the held crash shot, throne gesture, rising smoke and close-up of the sticks to cover the four handoffs. Preserve musical intros and tails. A camera cue that runs late can cut directly to the next opening pose.
- Run each song’s scoring through its final playable note and carry the result forward in the background. Keep the drum highway readable across cuts, especially during the fall, the explosion and the flashing rescue lights. Restore normal lane visibility before every playable entrance.
- Bind cues to sections in the selected recordings once the charts are available. In particular, check how much solo remains after the landing in hell and how much lead-in Heart’s drummer gives the player. Playtest all four boundaries in one run and check that the first note after each handoff is visible and audible on time.

**Open decision — interruption and resume.** A one-sitting version would restart at the chase after the player leaves. A resumable version could reopen at a song boundary or at a saved position within a song. Song-boundary resume would restore that song’s opening tableau and the earlier results. Mid-song resume would also need to restore the matching audio position, chart and score state, camera sequence, and current condition of the band, then give the player a playable lead-in. Decide the checkpoint granularity after playing the full sequence. In every version, a player who keeps playing moves straight into the next song.
