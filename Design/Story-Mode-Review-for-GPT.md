# Story mode: review notes and next pass

Notes on `Design/Story-Mode-Setlists.md`, for its next revision. The twelve sets, the recurring characters (the mustard-scarf commuter, the drilled loonie, the bucket) and the rewards that come home with you are the right direction. Keep all of that. What follows is where the doc doesn't yet fit the game, the band-building arc it needs to carry, and a format the game can load.

## 1. What the staging notes are for

The doc's per-song staging belongs to the **gig venue**, the stage you play on under the note highway. It is not the hideout, the front-end room that serves as the main menu. Say so in the doc's intro. The model is:

- **One venue per set, changing across its five songs.** Between songs the room changes state: the dessert table moves, the pool table gets covered, the pledge slips climb the glass. Each song can also have a cue inside it. The state persists, so returning for song four puts the room back the way song three left it.
- **Rewards go home to the hideout.** The loonie on the hi-hat stand, the mirror ball on the garage ceiling, the ON AIR lamp: each reward is a hideout item with a place in a room.
- **One exception.** Set 01, *In the Basement with My AirPods*, can be played in the hideout's basement scene itself. The basement is already built and dressed, with the kit, the window well and the stairs. Rewrite set 01's staging for that room. Its washer and laundry table would be new props.

## 2. How the game actually works today

Facts the next pass has to respect:

- **Camera.** The venue is shot by a director, who cuts between wide shots, band close-ups, the crowd and a guitar-neck camera. It is not the drummer's point of view, and the highway covers the lower middle of the screen. "Keep each change in the drummer's sightline" should become *"stage each change where a wide or crowd shot will catch it, and time it to a held groove, a rest or the end of a song."*
- **What people can do.** Band members are fully posed characters (they play, walk to and from the mic, headbang, clap). Crowd figures are simpler: they stand, bob, jump, sway, clap and fist-pump, keener toward the front. Fine actions such as writing on a transfer, mouthing a hook or signing a drumhead won't read on screen. Express a beat as **props that move, appear or light up, a character who walks to a new spot, a light change, or the crowd's mood changing.** The fine detail can live in the flavour text.
- **What a cue can hang on.** The chart's sections (intro, verse, chorus, solo, bridge, outro), the crowd mood markers, a guitar solo, the song's end, and the player's streak or misses. "On the first chorus" and "at the final hit" are usable. "When the bass drops low" is only usable if it lines up with a section.
- **Characters.** There are 8 character bodies × 12 clothing variants, about 96 looks. Every named person has to be one of those looks. Recurring characters (the commuter, the daughter, the kid in the big sweater) should keep the same look each time they appear.
- **Difficulty.** The game plays each chart's Expert drums only. Easier or reduced charts ("sparse kit arrangements", "a playable single-pedal reduction on the story chart") don't exist. Difficulty comes from song choice, measured as notes per second. Rewrite the playing notes to describe what the song already asks of a drummer, and put the real ramp in the song order. Bespoke story charts can go on a wishlist at the end, marked as optional.
- **Charts available.** Only songs with drum charts can be played. Of the 60 picks, these 5 are in the library now: *Misery Business*, *Scream Aim Fire*, *Collapsing*, *This Suffering*, *Everlong*. The rest need charts first. Other charted songs already in the library: *The Kill* (30 Seconds To Mars), *Hot* (Avril Lavigne), *Cure for the Enemy* (Billy Talent), *The Quiet Things That No One Ever Knows* (Brand New), *Lithonia* (Childish Gambino), *Dreaming* and *Last Goodbye* (Dead by April), *Here We Go Again* (Demi Lovato), *Learn to Fly* (Foo Fighters), *Brain Stew / Jaded* (Green Day), *Any Way You Want It* (Journey), *Black Betty* (Ram Jam), *While My Guitar Gently Weeps* (The Beatles), *Vicarious* (Tool), *More Than a Feeling* (Boston). For every slot, give **two alternates** from the Spotify export, and prefer charted songs when one fits.
- **Library songs are never locked.** Story mode gates the next set, never a song in free play. The doc's rule already does this. State it outright.
- **How a set is passed.** The current gig system plays a set back to back and judges the **average accuracy** of all its songs. Leaving partway abandons the gig. The doc's banking idea (finish songs one at a time, come back tomorrow) is better and will be built. Give each set a pass mark (for example, average accuracy ≥ 70%) so it's clear what banking a song means.
- **The four rooms.** The hideout moves from basement to garage to rehearsal space to studio. The rewards already hint at the right points: the garage key after set 04, the rented rehearsal room at set 07, the studio by set 11. Make each move explicit: a set's reward moves the band into the next room. The hideout is how the story shows progress, not a system to design (see section 6).

## 3. The missing arc: building the band

The doc follows the drummer alone. The campaign is also about **assembling a band**, and the people in it live in the hideout between gigs. The rules:

- **No hiring and no random pool.** Each band seat is filled at an **audition**, a story moment where the player chooses between **2 or 3 written candidates**. Each has a name, a personality, a genre they lean toward, a look from the 96 and a line or two of voice. After choosing, the player can adjust the member's look.
- **Rejected candidates come back.** One might sit in the touring band on the Steel Mill date, another in a rival act or the crowd at Maple Leaf Gardens. Pay these off in the staging.
- **Missing members are a feature.** If a seat is empty, nobody stands there. A solo set on a subway platform should look solo.
- **Hired-gun sets.** On some sets the drummer fills in for someone else's band (the radio station's MCs, the five acts on the Steel Mill tour). Those players are authored and fixed, not chosen.

A proposed mapping onto your sets. Push back where the story wants something else:

| Act | Sets | On stage | Hideout room | Audition at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Alone | 01 Basement, 02 Busking, 03 Coffee Shop (the barista sits in as a guest) | drummer | basement | **Bass.** The "BASSIST WANTED" flyer on the basement gig board pays off here. Could the barista be one of the candidates? |
| 2. Garage band | 04 Dive Bar (first gig with a bass amp), 05 Funeral Afterparty, 06 Roller Rink | + bass, then + guitar | garage (key from 04) | **Guitar** before 05; **vocals** after 06 |
| 3. Hired gun | 07 Pledge Drive, 08 Food Court, 09 FM Drive-In, 10 Steel Mill | your band on 08; session or fill-in on 07, 09 and 10 | rehearsal space (from 07) | optional **keys** |
| 4. Headliner | 11 Maple Leaf Gardens, 12 Stadium | your full band | studio | none: credits |

Set 11's line *"for the first time, the promoter asks what your band wants to play"* only lands if the band has been assembled on screen. This arc is what sets it up.

## 4. The venues

There are twelve bespoke venues, so mark how much each one costs to build:

- **Reuse.** Say which existing rooms each venue can be a dressed variant of: the four hideout rooms (basement, garage, rehearsal, studio) and the four stage rooms (Backyard, Club, Festival, Arena). The Dive Bar and the Legion can probably share a Club shell; the Stadium is the Arena opened up.
- **The kit's look.** Name what the drummer plays in each set: the kit, a bucket plus a practice pad on the subway, a house kit at the Steel Mill. The chart is the same kit chart either way; only the model changes.
- **The persistent state.** List what the venue remembers between songs, so it can be stored as flags: `dessert_table_moved`, `pool_table_covered`, `banner_dropped`.

## 6. Decisions already made

1. **The story sets are the campaign. The four existing gigs stay** (Backyard Party, Club Night, Festival Slot, Arena Headline) as side gigs with generated setlists, separate from the story. The venues already built (Backyard, Club, Festival, Arena) stay too: for free play, as shells for story venues (section 4), and for possible side beats later. Feel free to suggest side beats that use them, but don't make them story requirements.
2. **The hideout is visual pacing, not a mechanic.** It shows how far the story has come: the room changes and rewards appear on the walls. Say where each room move lands in the story (the garage after 04, the rehearsal space after 07, the studio by 11 is a good start), but don't design hideout mechanics. Those come later.
3. **The finale stays long.** Set 12 runs about 29 minutes and ends on *Free Bird*. It's the finale, so the length is the point. Pace it as an endurance set and write it to earn that length. Banking songs means it doesn't have to be played in one sitting.
4. **Maple Leaf Gardens keeps its real name.** Lean into the Canadian setting: the loonie, the Legion and the Gardens all stay.
